Privacy Policy

Zing (formerly Exam Helper) provides study guides, quizzes, flashcards, your own decks with Magic import, school leaderboards, Live games, challenges and study groups. This policy explains what is saved on your device, what goes online, what other students can see, and the controls available to you.

The Google sign-in screen may still show the app’s earlier name, ExamForge, until that setting is renamed. Questions about this app or your information can be sent to 06joshuaandersone4798@gmail.com.

Effective date: 7 November 2026.

1. Studying on your device

The app uses your browser’s local storage for study progress and settings. This includes topics visited, quiz results and mistakes, flashcard review levels, practice activity, XP and streaks, coins and power-ups, the decks you create (their cards, notes and subdecks) and your XP on each deck, and preferences such as theme, grade, sounds, music, haptics, font size and Do not disturb. Browser storage also keeps sign-in sessions, your setup answers while you are still setting up, and some interface settings. Temporary information, such as a live-game participant ID or a pending sign-in, can be held in session storage.

A service worker caches app files and study resources so previously loaded parts of the site can work offline. Device data belongs to that browser and website address; another browser or website address does not automatically have the same saved data.

2. Setting up your account

New users answer a few questions once: age, what brings them to the app (school, university, work or something else), school year, school, whether to turn on study reminders and friend finding, and first and last name. When online features are switched on, these answers are saved to your account. The app stores a birth year worked out from the age you enter, not your full date of birth. A username is made for you from your first name and last initial and can be changed in Account.

If you joined through a friend’s invite link, the account records who invited you so the inviter can receive in-app coins. Coins have no money value.

On a copy of the app without online features, the setup answers are kept only on your device.

3. Online accounts and cloud saves

Online features use Supabase for authentication, database storage and live-game connections. You can continue as a guest (an account without an email or password), sign in with Google, or sign in with an email link. Your profile stores your username, built-in avatar, grade, school, the setup answers above, league and XP activity (including XP per day, for monthly boards), when you were last active, and your notification choices. The online system also stores following relationships, school-library sets you publish, saved-set activity, Live challenges, study-group membership and messages, and account-transfer and request-limit records.

When cloud backup is used, the app sends a copy of your study-progress state and your decks to your account. This supports restoring or combining progress on another device. A deck’s cards and notes (including notes made from files, recordings or web pages you imported) are part of that backup, even when you have not shared the deck. If your notes are very long, the backup may keep only the newest ones in full.

Signing in with Google or email

If you choose Google sign-in, Google supplies basic identity information to Supabase Auth, such as your account identifier, name, email address and profile information. The Google sign-in configuration is limited to basic identity permissions: openid, email and profile. The app does not request permission to read Gmail, Google Drive, contacts or calendars. If you choose email sign-in, your email address is sent to Supabase Auth, which emails you a one-time sign-in link.

Your email address and your Google name are not shown to other students. You can review or remove the Google connection in your Google Account connections; doing so does not itself remove data already stored in Zing.

4. Magic import and Zing AI

Files are read on your device. When you import a PDF, Word or PowerPoint file, a text file or a spreadsheet, the app reads it in your browser; the file itself is not uploaded. Quizlet, Anki and spreadsheet text becomes cards on your device without any AI. Without Zing AI, the app can also make simple “quick cards” from your notes on your device.

Zing AI writes cards (questions, answers, wrong answers and short explanations) from study material you choose. It is available to students who are 14 or older by birth year and signed in with Google or a confirmed email address (not guest accounts), with a daily limit for each student and for everyone. When you use it, the material you picked is sent through Zing’s Supabase server function to Cloudflare Workers AI, which runs the Gemma 4 model (and Whisper to turn a recording into text). That material can be: text from your notes or a file (up to about 24,000 characters), up to 4 photos (made smaller first), a short recording, or the address of a web page, which Zing’s server then fetches. Your name, email address, school and sign-in token are not sent to Cloudflare. Cloudflare states that it does not use this content to train AI models and normally keeps it only to answer the request. Zing’s server does not store the material and does not log its contents; it keeps a count of how many imports each account used that day, to apply the limits.

The cards you get back, and a copy of the text (your notes, the recording’s transcript or the web page’s text) as a note, are saved in your deck. Photos and recordings are not kept after the cards are made. Only import study material: leave out photos of people and personal details such as names, phone numbers or addresses. AI-made cards can be wrong, so check them; you can edit, delete or undo them.

“Try a sample” sends one of two built-in sample texts, not anything you wrote.

5. What other people can see

  • Profiles and leaderboards: your username, selected avatar, grade, school, league, XP, ranks, follower/following counts, and whether you are active right now can appear to other visitors.
  • Your name: if you are 13 or older and friend finding is on, classmates at your school can find you by name and see your first name and last initial. Your last name, age, email and setup answers are never shown to other students. With friend finding off, you only appear by username.
  • Suggestions: classmates with friend finding on can be suggested to each other. People who follow you can see you in their friend lists.
  • Live challenges: when you challenge someone, they see your username, avatar, the subject or topic and the game code for 20 minutes. You can challenge people who follow you, and classmates aged 13 or older with friend finding on.
  • Study groups: group members see the group name, the other members’ usernames, avatars and (where allowed) first name and last initial, and the messages sent. Messages can only be emoji, ready-made phrases, cards from the app’s decks and Live game codes; groups have no free typing.
  • Shared decks: sharing a deck while signed in uploads its name, colour and cards (not your notes) so anyone with the link can view and save it. It shows your username, and your school if you also list it in your school library (off unless you tick it). You can see how many times your deck was saved, and you get in-app coins for each save. Without an account, a share link carries the deck’s cards inside the link itself.
  • Deck leaderboards: when you study a shared deck (yours or one you saved), your XP on that deck, username, avatar and (where allowed) first name and last initial appear on that deck’s leaderboard for anyone who has it.
  • School libraries: listing a deck in your school library makes its title and cards available with your username and school. Settings has an option to share new decks with your school library automatically; it is off unless you turn it on.
  • Invite links: an invite link contains your username. Anyone who receives a link can use it and forward it.
  • Live games: participants use a game code to connect through Supabase Realtime and exchange names, built-in avatars, presence, the game name, questions, answers and response times, scores and reactions. Anyone with the game code can try to join.

Keep private information out of usernames, study-group names, deck names and cards, game names and shared links.

6. Students under 13 (and under 14 for Zing AI)

If you are under 13, friend finding stays off, your name is never shown to other students, you are not suggested to classmates, and study groups are not available. Zing AI is only for students 14 and older by birth year; younger students can still make decks, write cards and use quick cards. You can still study, play Live games with a code, and appear on your school’s board by username. Under-13s can only be challenged by people they follow.

7. Profile pictures and GIFs

A custom photo or animated GIF you select is processed in your browser and saved on that device. It is not included in the app’s cloud progress/set backup and is not sent as your public picture. Other people see your selected built-in avatar. You can remove the custom picture in the picture editor or clear the site’s browser data.

8. Reminders and notifications

Study reminders appear inside the app when your streak needs attention, unless Do not disturb is on. Card reminder notifications use your browser’s notification permission, which you can grant or refuse; they show while the app is open in a browser tab. The app does not send marketing email.

9. External services

EdgeOne and Vercel host and deliver the website. The app also loads fonts and some software, icons, 3D components and the PDF reader (PDF.js) through Google Fonts, cdnjs/Cloudflare and jsDelivr. Zing AI uses Cloudflare Workers AI, as described in section 4. These services receive the network requests needed to provide those resources, which can include connection information such as an IP address and browser/request details.

School searches use the app’s Supabase school directory. Choosing Search the world map sends the school-name query and selected country to the Overpass API for an OpenStreetMap lookup. Choosing to add a school submits the school name, country and any region information you enter; map-based records may include the school’s map location. This lookup does not request your device’s location.

The app also links to educational reference websites. Opening those links takes you to the relevant website, which handles your visit under its own policies.

10. Your controls and deletion

  • Edit your account: change your name, age, username, school, grade and avatar in Account; switch friend finding, reminders and other preferences in Settings.
  • Leave a study group: use the leave button in the group. A group with no members left is deleted.
  • Remove a shared deck: deleting the deck while signed in also removes its shared copy and its leaderboard. Copies already saved by other people may remain with them.
  • Sign out: signs you out in this browser. It keeps local study progress, decks and your custom picture, and shows the sign-in screen again.
  • Reset progress: resets the app’s study-progress state. It does not by itself remove your decks, custom picture or account.
  • Clear site data: your browser’s site-data controls can remove local storage and cached files. This can also remove access to a guest account on that device. Clearing browser data does not delete the cloud account.
  • Delete your account: Settings → Delete account removes your profile and setup answers, cloud save, following relationships, XP history, Live challenges, study-group memberships and the messages you sent, shared decks and their leaderboards, your places on other decks’ leaderboards, saved-deck records and transfer-code record. Zing AI’s daily counts are cleared each day. It does not clear device data, remove contributed school-directory entries, or automatically delete the underlying Supabase Auth sign-in record.

For questions about information associated with your account, removal requests, or a problem using these controls, contact 06joshuaandersone4798@gmail.com. Keep account-transfer codes private: possession of a valid code can move the account.

Device information remains until the applicable in-app control removes it or your browser clears it. Cloud data is separate from browser storage and is affected by the cloud deletion controls described above. Live challenges expire after 20 minutes and stop being shown, and are deleted with your account. This policy does not specify a fixed retention period for hosting, authentication or service logs, or deletion of third-party backups.
